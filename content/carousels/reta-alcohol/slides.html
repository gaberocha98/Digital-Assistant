Mechanism 1

It starts in your stomach

Retatrutide slows gastric emptying. Food and alcohol linger longer before moving on.

That shifts how alcohol enters your bloodstream — so many people feel intoxicated on far less than they used to.

Mechanism 2

The enzyme connection

Research on GLP-1 receptor agonists points to reduced activity of a key alcohol-metabolizing enzyme.

Break alcohol down more slowly, and your blood alcohol climbs higher — and stays elevated longer.

Reported in npj Metabolic Health & Disease

Mechanism 3

Weight loss quietly lowers tolerance

Less body mass means less body water. The same drink now spreads through a smaller volume — pushing your blood alcohol concentration higher.

You didn't change. Your body did.
